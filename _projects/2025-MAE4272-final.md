**Wind Turbine Blade Design Project (MAE 4272)**

**Project Overview**

This project focused on the design, fabrication, and experimental evaluation of small-scale wind turbine blades optimized for safe operation and power extraction under constrained operating conditions. The blades were designed to operate near a target rotational speed of 1500 RPM at a mean free-stream velocity of 4.59 m/s, as informed by a Weibull wind-speed distribution. Key constraints included a maximum allowable rotational speed of 2000 RPM, a fixed hub radius of 1 inch, and a maximum blade length of 6 inches. Safety was a primary focus throughout our project.

**Design Process**

The design process emphasized risk mitigation and aerodynamic efficiency. Two dominant failure modes, flapwise bending fracture and torque brake failure, were identified through prior lab work and analysis. To maintain acceptable factors of safety against both, a conservative blade length of 4 inches was selected. Aerodynamic optimization was performed using the NACA 4412 airfoil at a Reynolds number of approximately 50,000. The blade twist distribution was designed to maintain an angle of attack of 8.5°, corresponding to the maximum lift-to-drag ratio for this airfoil.

**Testing and Results**

Experimental testing was conducted in a wind tunnel at three free-stream velocities: 4.3 m/s, 5.5 m/s, and 6.1 m/s. Torque and power were measured as functions of rotational speed using a controlled torque brake. Peak power occurred below the original design target of 1500 RPM at approximately 800 RPM, 1100 RPM, and 1250 RPM for the three wind speeds, respectively. While the magnitude of measured power wasn't comparable to theoretical predictions, at lower rotational speeds actual behavior trends closely matched the theoretical patterns. These behaviors diverged at higher rotational speeds due to unmodeled internal friction. No structural damage or deformation was observed during testing.
